Welcome to DS 2023¶

This course introduces students to the theory and practice of visualizing data as both a research tool and communication strategy. The course is divided into two broad parts: data visualization and storytelling with visualizations.

In the first part of the course, we focus on how to create plots. This includes related topics such as data types and scale, tables and data frames, in addition to core Python visualization libraries, elementary and advanced plot types, and exploratory data analysis (EDA). The emphasis is on developing your media fluency to create the kinds of plots data scientists use in their work to understand and model data. We will also pause to survey some of the visualization techniques used for the broader world of data types you will encounter as a data scientist, including temporal, spatial, textual, and networked data.

In the second part of the course, we turn our attention to communicating the results of research through composition and storytelling. Here, the visualizations of the first part become the building blocks for creating presentations in various form, from static “infographics” to interactive visualization, in a variety of contexts, from scientific writing to journalism. Again, the focus is on developing your media fluency to create things. In addition, we develop our ability to critically examine how visual and narrative choices shape meaning and persuasion, and how to both avoid and detect misleading representations of data and findings.
